The invitation is finished, the link works, and you send it. Then half the family says they never got it.

They did. It arrived in a group at 11pm between a forwarded video and someone's good-morning image, and it scrolled away. This is a delivery problem, not an invitation problem, and it is mostly fixable with how you send.

## Never send a bare link

A URL on its own reads as a forward. Forwards get ignored, particularly by the older relatives whose attendance you care most about.

Two or three lines is enough. Name yourselves, say what it is, say what you want:

> Namaste Uncle, Aunty —
> Ananya and Rohan are getting married on 14 February, and we would be so happy to have you with us.
> Here are all the details, including the schedule and directions:
> [link]
> Do let us know how many of you will be able to come.

That takes twenty seconds longer to send and roughly triples the chance of a response.

<Callout type="tip" title="Send it from the right person">
An invitation from the bride's mother to her own siblings lands very differently from the same link forwarded by a cousin. For the guests who matter most, it is worth the sender being the person they expect to hear from.
</Callout>

## Order of sending

<Steps>
<Step title="Close family, individually, first">
Before anything goes into a group. Nobody in the immediate family should learn the date from a group forward.
</Step>
<Step title="Family groups next">
This is your reach. Post it with a message, not alone, and post it at a time people are awake — mid-morning or early evening, not midnight.
</Step>
<Step title="Friends and colleagues after that">
Individually or in small groups. Colleagues in particular respond better to a direct message than to being added to something.
</Step>
<Step title="A second wave about a month before">
Same link, short message: "Just a reminder — the wedding is in four weeks and here are the details again." People forget. This is normal and this message is not an imposition.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Check the preview before you send it

When you paste the link, WhatsApp fetches a preview card — a photo, the couple's names, a short description. Send it to yourself first and look at what appears.

If the preview shows a plain grey box or the wrong photograph, fix that before the link goes anywhere. The preview is what most people register in a busy group; a link with a good card gets opened far more than one without.

<Callout type="warning" title="Previews cache">
WhatsApp remembers the preview it fetched the first time a link was shared. If you change the photo afterwards, older chats may keep showing the old card for a while. Get the preview right before the first send rather than after.
</Callout>

## Groups: a few practical notes

- **Do not create a new group for the wedding** unless you intend to run it. An unmanaged wedding group becomes a support queue.
- **Pin the message** in family groups you control, so the link stays reachable when the conversation moves on.
- **Expect the link to be forwarded onward.** It will reach people you did not send it to. If that is a problem, use personalised links per family rather than one shared URL.

## What to send on the day itself

One short message on the morning of each function, to the people attending it, with the timing and the venue link. Not the full invitation again — just the thing they need in the next two hours.

> Today's sangeet starts at 7:30pm at Hotel Suryagarh. Directions: [link]. See you there!

This is the message guests are most grateful for, and it is the one almost nobody sends.
